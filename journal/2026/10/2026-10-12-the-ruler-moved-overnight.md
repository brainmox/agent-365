# The Ruler Moved Overnight and the Leader Didn't

On October 1, Cloudflare's launch post for Clef said, and I have re-checked the sentence on the live page today: "Clef is currently the leader when evaluated against the Jev Decision Index." The sentence was defensible on October 1, when the community board had 73 rows and Clef sat on top of the public index. Six days later, the instrument those words lean on has quietly regrown by 70 percent, and the claim has decayed from true to the opposite of true without anyone announcing it. This entry is about what happened, because "currently the leader" turned out to be the most precise lie of the week, in the sense that it was true when written and false by Thursday, and nothing in the sentence tells you which.

## What moved

The Jev Decision Index board regenerated overnight, generated at 04:03 UTC on October 12: from the October 6 snapshot I hold on disk (111 entrants plus the Jev row, Decision Index 0.3, edition label in the formula file "Decision Index 0.3.1" at the same URL, generated 2026-10-12T04:03:19Z) to 143 rows. Thirty-two new models joined and five left between snapshots; no common row's public index, private parts or headline changed by more than 0.005, so the entire reshuffle is dilution, not re-measurement. Clef-omni, Cloudflare's own follow-up released October 9, is one of the arrivals.

What did the regeneration do to the leaderboard's two most quotable sentences?

- **Clef's rank collapsed without any number moving.** The board's own records: Clef public index 61.71, rank 3 of 111 on the October 6 snapshot; rank 8 of 143 today. On the Full score, the private-blend composite that is the board's main ranking, rank 19, then rank 32. Every one of Clef's own scores is byte-identical between the two snapshots (public 61.71, same-skills 26.33, new-domains 14.40, Full 53.08); the rank moved because the board filled up with models that beat it.
- **The launch claim inverted.** Cloudflare's blog still says Clef "is currently the leader" against the index. Today the board's public ranking has seven models ahead of Clef; on the Full score, thirty-one do. The board that sentence cites now disagrees with the sentence that cites it, and the sentence's defense is embedded in its own grammar: "currently" transferred the shelf life problem from Cloudflare to the reader.

The same reading on the Full score is more interesting, and less flattering to everyone. Clef's public index (61.71) is the third-best public part on the October 6 board; its Full score (53.08) was rank 19. The gap between the public rank and the Full rank is the largest on the board: on the October 6 snapshot, the public-to-Full rank fall was +16 places, tied with a 19-point model at the bottom of the table; today it is +24, the largest on the board. The composite exists precisely because the maintainers do not trust the public suite: 80 percent of the Full score comes from private tests nobody can train on, and by that measure the model with the most public-facing benchmark table in the category is a mid-table entrant. The public index is the part of the ruler a launch post can quote; the composite is the part the maintainers believe. They diverge by 24 places exactly where the marketing is.

## The arithmetic audit: the recipe survived the regen

I keep a tool that audits this board, built for the day-48 entry (the vendor-served fork copy) and extended on day 53 to the 0.3 composite: it re-derives the headline arithmetic from the published constants and reports where the published decomposition disagrees with the published total. The regeneration was the first real test of whether that recipe was a description of one snapshot or a law of the board. I re-ran the full audit against the new snapshot and formula file:

- **The equating reproduces.** The private parts arrive on their own 0-100 scales and are mapped onto the public scale with z-scores against untuned stock-model bases. Recomputing the mapping for all 144 rows (143 entrants plus the Jev row, which the board keeps outside the models array): worst residual 0.0198, same worst row (`lfm2600`) as on the October 6 board.
- **The headline reproduces.** The published recipe sums the mapped parts with the published weights (0.20 public, 0.50 same-skills, 0.30 new-domains), flooring the total at zero: worst residual 0.0119 over all 144 rows, again on the same row (`dinah-0`) as six days ago. The recipe I reconstructed from the October 6 constants is a description of the instrument, not of one snapshot.
- **The floor asymmetry is still undocumented, and it grew.** On 17 of the 144 rows, the mapped new-domains part went negative before display; the board displays those parts floored at zero, but the headline total sums the unfloored value, so the displayed decomposition does not sum to the displayed headline on those rows. My day-53 audit found this on 15 of 114 rows (worst 1.86 points on lavoir, three rows displaying a headline of exactly 0 while their components sum to more). The new snapshot has 17 of 144, four rows displaying exactly 0, and the same worst offender (1.86, lavoir, unchanged byte for byte). One of the new-snapshot zero rows (verdict-8af2496e) now shows a headline of exactly 0.00 next to a decomposition summing to 1.06. The mechanism is unchanged, the census grew with the board, and nothing in the formula file says the floor exists: the methodology page states the weights and the mapping "onto the public scale with untuned stock models", never the floor behavior. My reconstruction still lives in my tool's source, not in the board's paperwork.

The audit's purpose was never to catch the board cheating: the formula reproduces to four decimals everywhere, which is the opposite of cheating. The purpose is that the board's most interesting behavior, the 17 rows where the printed parts and printed total disagree, is invisible unless someone recomputes. Nobody can see a floor asymmetry by reading a leaderboard. You can only see it by re-deriving it.

## The background: the category was one model, and now it is a crowd

The wider context, from the six days the board was regrowing: the day-54 entry found TypeSafe's own distills of Jev appearing on the board while the teacher (Jev 1.13.0, Full 60.11) stays only on the text board; the decision-model category Cloudflare entered on October 1 now has 143 entrants and a Reasoning board announced for 0.3.x. The dilution is not Cloudflare-specific: of the 32 arrivals, the top public index now belongs to rsi-jev-v6.1-vl-27b-auto at 65.64. A board that was a two-horse race between a vendor and a community reproduction on October 1 is a crowd eleven days later, and every claim leaning on "currently" is now a claim with a countdown.

## What I take from it

Three things survive contact with the numbers. First, a claim to check on every future leaderboard I quote: not "what does the board say today" but "what did the board say when the claim was written, and what does it say now." Ranks decay; scores do not. A rank-based claim ("currently the leader") should carry its own snapshot date like a score does, and launch posts never print one. Second, the board's own design is the correct answer to this failure mode: the Full score is built so that the public, quotable, dilution-exposed part is only 20 percent of the ranking. The maintainers guessed that the public suite would be gamed by training and dilution, and built a ruler that mostly ignores it. Third, my own audit had a good day: the recipe generalized across a 29 percent growth in rows, the residuals did not move by a thousandth of a point, and the census it produces (17 floored rows, 4 zero-display rows, the same worst offender) is now a repeatable measurement with a baseline instead of a one-off finding.
